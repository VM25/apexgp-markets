# Audit of ApexGP Markets v2 (June–July 2026)

Scope: the code, data, documentation and deployed behaviour of the repository at tag `legacy-v2-june-2026`. Every finding below was verified by reading the file cited; file paths are relative to that tag (`git show legacy-v2-june-2026:<path>`). Existing documentation was treated as a set of claims to check, not as fact.

Severity: **A** invalidates results or misleads the user · **B** materially wrong · **C** weak but not misleading.

## 1. Data

| # | Sev | Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | A | The "official" race files were generated by a script, not sourced. | `scripts/generate_official_race_data.py` builds every lap from constants. `public/data/official/` is its output. |
| D2 | A | Starting grids are invented: the eventual winner is placed on pole and the rest ordered by fabricated points. | `generate_race_data`: `sorted_drivers = [winner_code] + non_winners_sorted`. |
| D3 | A | Safety cars are a formula: lap 18 at Monaco, lap 35 in every third round, none otherwise. | `sc_lap = 18 if race_id == "monaco" else (35 if round_number % 3 == 0 else None)`. The real 2025 record (OpenF1) has 17 safety-car and 13 virtual-safety-car deployments at entirely different times. |
| D4 | A | Retirements are a formula: Stroll on lap 44 in even rounds, Ocon on lap 31 in odd rounds, always "engine blowout". | `dnf_driver = "STR" if round_number % 2 == 0 else "OCO"`. |
| D5 | A | Per-race points are a driver's season total spread evenly over the races they did not win. | `solve_points_matrix()`; comment: "mathematically perfect points matrix". |
| D6 | B | Season totals are right only for the top seven drivers; the other thirteen are invented (e.g. Sainz 135, Albon 12; real: 64 and 73). Constructors' totals inherit the error. | `TARGET_DRIVERS_POINTS`. Verified against Jolpica final standings. |
| D7 | B | The entry list is wrong: Lawson is at Red Bull and Doohan at Alpine all season, Verstappen carries #33, Colapinto never appears. | `DRIVERS` constant. |
| D8 | B | Every race has the same pit stops (top four on lap 20), constant 1.6 s gaps that never change, constant weather, and the first race is 58 laps (it ran 57). | `generate_race_data`. |
| D9 | B | `scripts/validate_official_data.py` validates the generated files against the same constants that generated them. | The check is circular. |
| D10 | C | A second synthetic dataset (`public/data/archive_synthetic/`) shipped alongside, unused. | Removed. |

**Consequence.** No market, replay or statistic in v2 reflected a real race. The winners were real; nothing else was.

**Resolution.** All generated data is deleted. `pipeline/` rebuilds every round from Jolpica F1 and OpenF1 with cross-source checks (`docs/DATA.md`).

## 2. Pricing

| # | Sev | Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | A | Look-ahead: every in-race price was a blend of a heuristic and the *known final result*. | `src/hooks/useSimulation.ts`, `repriceMarketsForLap`: `const actualOut = (finalState.position === 1 && !finalState.dnf) ? 1.0 : 0.0; winC.mid = (1 - w) * stateProb + w * actualOut`, with `w = (lap / totalLaps) ** 1.6`. The same pattern for podium, head-to-head and fastest lap. The code's own comment calls `w` the "information leakage / outcome convergence factor". |
| P2 | A | This was documented as a "Bayesian Repricing Engine" and a posterior. A weighted average with the outcome is not a posterior of anything. | `docs/v2/README.md` §1. |
| P3 | A | The fastest-lap market settled on whoever started third: `finalFL = data.starting_grid[2]?.driver_code`. | Same file, twice. |
| P4 | B | Prior probabilities were hard-coded with hindsight: title futures started at VER 44%, NOR 38%, LEC 12%, PIA 5%, HAM 1%, for five drivers only. | `initializeContracts`. |
| P5 | B | Title prices were each driver's share of points among five names, floored at 4¢; the title settled on the largest total *among those five*. | `repriceMarketsForLap` §6. Walk-forward scoring shows this heuristic has roughly three times the Brier score of a season simulation (`docs/METHODOLOGY.md` §3.4). |
| P6 | B | Prices were normalised to sum to 0.96 (winner) and 2.88 (podium) and that sum was then called the mid: an overround baked into "fair value". | `normalizeContracts(…, 0.96)`. |
| P7 | B | The retirement price depended on tyre age; the safety-car price drifted upward every lap regardless of state. | `dnfC.mid = 0.02 + tire_age * 0.003 + …`. |
| P8 | B | "Delta", "Theta" and "implied volatility" were `mid × (1 − mid) × constant` and similar. There is no model in which these are sensitivities. | `calculateGreeks`. |

**Resolution.** A fitted, validated model with look-ahead excluded by construction and by test; the fastest-lap market is removed; Greeks are removed (`docs/METHODOLOGY.md`).

## 3. Market and accounting

| # | Sev | Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | B | The spread was mid ± 2¢ for every contract in every state. No depth, no size limits, no fees, no price impact. | `c.bid = mid - 0.02; c.ask = mid + 0.02`. |
| M2 | B | "Trading halts" were a `setTimeout` of 2.5–3.5 s of wall-clock time after an event, independent of playback speed. | `setIsHalted(true); setTimeout(…, 3500)`. |
| M3 | A | Replay exploit: resetting or finishing a race and pressing play again re-ran the same race with the account intact. A user could buy the known winner at the grid price repeatedly. | `handlePlayPause` resets the lap to 0 when the race is over; `usePortfolio` keeps cash. |
| M4 | A | Switching race or resetting liquidated open positions at the last recorded bid — including positions in a contract whose outcome was by then visible. | `settleAllActivePositions(true)` in the race-switch and reset effects. |
| M5 | B | Settlement ran inside a React effect keyed on the lap counter and was not idempotent by design; correctness depended on render timing. | `useEffect(() => { if (currentLap === totalLaps) settleAllActivePositions(false) })`. |
| M6 | B | State was not persisted: a reload restored $100,000 and discarded all positions. | No storage calls anywhere. |
| M7 | B | Exposure limits (30% total, 15% per market) summed position values as if contracts were independent; a winner, podium and points position on one driver counted as three unrelated risks. | `buyContracts`. |
| M8 | B | "Sharpe ratio" was the mean over standard deviation of lap-to-lap returns multiplied by √252. Laps are not trading days. | `getSharpeRatio`. |
| M9 | C | Entry prices were rounded to three decimals after averaging, so cash and basis could drift apart. | `entryPrice: Math.round(newEntry * 1000) / 1000`. |
| M10 | C | All of pricing, replay, UI tab state and settlement lived in one 1,069-line React hook; none of it was unit-tested. | `useSimulation.ts`; no test files in the repository. |

**Resolution.** A forward-only session clock, idempotent settlement, an integer-cent ledger with tested invariants, joint-scenario risk, persistence, and a dealer with state-dependent spread, depth, inventory and fees (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`, `docs/METHODOLOGY.md`).

## 4. Documentation claims that did not hold

| Claim (`docs/v2`, old README) | Reality |
|---|---|
| "the official 2025 Formula One World Championship season" / "real race telemetry" | Generated (D1–D8) |
| "Bayesian Repricing Engine" | Blend toward the known outcome (P1–P2) |
| "Synthetic Automated Market Maker" | A constant ±2¢ (M1) |
| "Option Greeks" | Unfounded formulas (P8) |
| "Sharpe Ratio annualized by √252" | Not a Sharpe ratio (M8) |
| "institutional risk desk limits" | Independent-sum caps (M7) |

## 5. Interface

The July redesign had already fixed the worst layout problems (body scroll, 7–10 px type, no breakpoints). Remaining issues at the audit: the six tabs were views onto one hook rather than a workflow; simulated prices, model numbers and (fabricated) race data shared one visual language; several explanations were hover-only; and nothing distinguished a replay from a live market. The v3 interface is new in every respect (`docs/DESIGN.md`).

## 6. What was worth keeping

- The idea: race events as information, binary contracts as the instrument, a portfolio as the consequence.
- The contract families (winner, podium, retirement, head-to-head, titles) and the $100,000 starting balance.
- Next.js, React, TypeScript and Tailwind as the application stack, and Netlify as the host.
- The project name.

Nothing else — no component, hook, style, dataset or document — was carried forward.
